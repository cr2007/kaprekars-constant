# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Curious casual visitors with no assumed math background who land on the page
and want a small moment of wonder. Students and educators may reuse it as a
demo, but the primary design target is someone trying it for the first time
with only basic arithmetic. The interface should never assume prior knowledge
of number theory.

## Product Purpose

An interactive visualizer for Kaprekar's routine. A visitor types any 4-digit
number, and the app walks through the arithmetic live: sort the digits high
to low and low to high, subtract, repeat, land on 6174. Success looks like a
visitor understanding *why* the trick works, not just seeing the fact stated,
and feeling curious enough to try another number.

## Positioning

It shows the math happening. Instead of stating that any 4-digit number
collapses to 6174, it animates every subtraction step live, so the visitor
watches the arithmetic converge rather than being told it does.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, precise. Friendly and inviting in tone and color, but the
arithmetic itself stays exact and legible; playfulness lives in the motion
and palette, never in fuzziness about the numbers.

## Anti-references

Explicitly not a generic AI-generated landing page: no identical card grids,
no gradient text, no tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows, no hero-metric
templates, no side-stripe borders. Not overly minimalist or clinical either;
flat gray/neutral UI with a single muted accent would undersell the "pop of
color" the product needs. No emojis, no em dashes, anywhere in copy.

## Design Principles

- Show, don't tell: every abstract fact about the routine must be visualized
  as it happens, not just stated in a label.
- Compute honestly: playful tone and color never blur the arithmetic; digits,
  operators, and results stay legible and exact at every step.
- Delight is earned by rarity: reserve the strongest celebratory motion and
  color intensity for the moment 6174 is reached, not scattered evenly across
  the page.
- One trick, done exceptionally: resist adding unrelated features or games;
  make this single interaction unforgettable instead.
- Accessible by default: legible contrast, keyboard-operable controls,
  reduced-motion support, and a real dark mode, without feeling clinical.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Requires dark mode support (not just an unstyled default
dark palette), meaningful alt text on any non-decorative imagery, and layouts
and language that stay friendly to someone with only basic math knowledge.
Reduced-motion is already implemented for entrance and celebration animations
and must be preserved through future changes.
